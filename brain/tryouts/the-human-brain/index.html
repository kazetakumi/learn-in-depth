The Human Brain

Goal — given a behavior or deficit, predict whether it points to a dedicated brain module vs. distributed processing, and say what evidence would tell them apart.
Standing on — the engineering intuition that you find out whether a system has a dedicated component by disabling it and watching what breaks.
  1. ✓
    One ability, or two?
    Bob loses navigation but nothing else — does that prove navigation is a separate piece of mind, or did the hardest task just break first?
  2. ✓
    Correlation in the brain isn't causation
    fMRI lights up for faces — that's a correlation. What would it take to show this tissue causes face recognition?
  3. ✓
    Does "responds more" mean "represents"?
    Haxby's attack on the FFA: it prefers faces, but its activity pattern still discriminates chairs from cars. Debunked?
  4. ▶
    When the framework should say "no module here"
    The 20-category scan found nothing for snakes, tools, flowers, food, cars — only faces/places/bodies/text replicate.
  5. ○
    Coda: where do modules come from?not load-bearing
    Bob never regained navigation as an adult. Children with equivalent damage often do.
